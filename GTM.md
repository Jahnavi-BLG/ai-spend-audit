# Go-To-Market (GTM) Strategy

## Exact Target Users
- **Bootstrapped Startup Founders:** They are extremely cash-conscious and looking to extend their runway by cutting $200-$500/mo in SaaS bloat.
- **Engineering Managers / Tech Leads:** They manage team licenses for tools like GitHub Copilot, ChatGPT, and Claude, but often lose track of who is actually using what.
- **Fractional CFOs:** Consultants who are hired to clean up company finances and need quick tools to audit software spend.

## Where They Hang Out Online
- **Twitter/X:** Tech communities under hashtags like `#buildinpublic`, `#indiehackers`, and `#SaaS`.
- **Reddit:** `r/startups`, `r/SaaS`, `r/Entrepreneur`, and `r/webdev`.
- **Communities:** IndieHackers forum, HackerNews, and private Slack/Discord communities for founders (e.g., Y Combinator's Startup School network).

## How to get the first 100 users with $0 budget

**1. The "Value-First" Reddit Strategy**
I wouldn't just post a link to my app. Instead, I'd do manual audits for people. I'll post on `r/startups` with a title like: *"I just audited $5,000/mo of AI software spend for a dev agency. Here's exactly where they were wasting money."* I'll break down the math, and at the very end, drop a link: *"If you want to check your own stack, I built a free calculator here: [Link]"*

**2. Direct Twitter/X Outreach**
I will search Twitter for phrases like "SaaS bills", "ChatGPT subscription", or "cutting costs". I'll reply to founders complaining about their burn rate with a friendly DM: *"Hey! I built a free tool that scans your AI stack for redundancies. Mind if I send you the link? Would love your feedback."*

**3. Product Hunt Launch**
Once the tool is polished, I'll schedule a Product Hunt launch. Since the tool is genuinely useful and free, it has a high chance of getting upvotes from the maker community. I'll frame it as: *"The ultimate AI subscription auditor for cash-strapped startups."*

**4. Engineering Blogs / Newsletters**
I'll reach out to mid-sized tech newsletters (like TLDR Web Dev or Bytes) and pitch them the tool as a free resource for their readers.
